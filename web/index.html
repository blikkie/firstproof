FIRST PROOF

An honest RAW photo editor.

I built this because I wanted a photo editor that was honest. It should show the world as it looks and shouldn’t require a million tweaks to look good—the first proof should come out right.

As a software engineer I generally know better than to do things that are unreasonable to vibe code, but I also like to take an idea, a cheap model, and see how far I can get.

Along the way, I gave myself a strict rule: no magic constants to make photos artificially look "good." Everything had to be grounded either in color science or darkroom chemistry. Because of that, I went a bit down the rabbit hole and spent a lot of innovation tokens on physical film and print media emulation.

Finally, I wanted to be less beholden to Adobe and take true ownership of my own photos. (Though to be fair, the library management component right now is rudimental, to put it kindly.)

Natural sky & water tones

Camera JPEGs often push blue skies toward cyan to make them look punchier. First Proof preserves the natural depth of the sky and water reflections without shifting hue angles.

True color, not neon saturation

Instead of boosting saturation across the board, colors retain their real-world character: the torii gate stays a deep architectural vermilion, blossoms keep subtle pinks, and foliage looks like real trees.

Clean, unsharpened detail

No artificial edge halos or heavy compression artifacts. Detail in the wood texture and distant branches comes from proper optical demosaicing rather than digital sharpening filters.

Principles

A Few Ground Rules

The constraints that guided how this editor was built.

Rule I

An Honest Baseline

A RAW photo should look right the moment you open it. If you need 40 micro-adjustments just to rescue an image from looking dull, the baseline color engine isn't doing its job.

Rule II

No Magic Constants

No arbitrary multipliers or curve hacks to force photos to look "pop." Every slider and tone model is tied to published optical physics, color science, or darkroom film chemistry.

Rule III

Fast Where It Matters

Flipping through hundreds of photos during a cull should feel instantaneous. Previews are generated fast with 2×2 Bayer binning so you don't wait on progress bars just to pick your best shots.

Rule IV

Your Files, Your Control

No cloud locks, no subscription checks, and no proprietary databases. Edits are saved in plain-text XMP sidecars alongside your photos, so your work stays yours forever.

Film & Paper Chemists

Kodak, Fujifilm, Ilford, and Agfa for decades of published sensitometric data and dye chemistry. Honestly, seeing what it takes to model physical film digitally mostly just makes me want to put down the laptop and take out an actual film camera.

AgX Filmic Color Science

Pioneering research on open-domain filmic tone mapping and highlight desaturation matrices in wide-gamut imagery.

Marti Maria

LittleCMS (lcms2)

Author and maintainer of LittleCMS, the open-source color engine powering ICC profile management and soft proofing.

Dave Coffin & RAW Maintainers

dcraw, libraw & rawler

Decades of reverse-engineering camera sensor formats, allowing photographers to read raw sensor data without proprietary software.

Torsten Bronger & Lensfun

Lens Calibration Database

A community-maintained database of optical distortion, vignetting, and chromatic aberration profiles for thousands of camera lenses.
